Karaoke Machine With Pitch Detection

Embedded Systems / FPGA

← Back to portfolio
Overview

This project focuses on designing and implementing a karaoke machine on the Intel DE1-SoC FPGA development platform using a Nios V processor. The system captures live microphone audio, performs pitch detection, compares the user’s singing pitch against target musical notes, displays lyrics and scoring information on a VGA monitor, and plays synchronized audio tracks through external speakers.

Design Motivation

Most karaoke systems primarily focus on audio playback and lyric display, but do not provide feedback on vocal accuracy. This project was designed to make karaoke systems more interactive and meaningful by analyzing the user’s singing pitch and providing a score based on how well they sang.

System Design

The system is divided into several interconnected subsystems responsible for audio processing, pitch analysis, graphics rendering, and user interaction. The design runs on the Intel DE1-SoC FPGA platform using a Nios V processor.

The design is best explained by the flowchart shown in Figure 1.

Figure 1. Shows the general block diagram of the design. Yellow circles indicate starting and ending blocks.

The user can control the system (select songs, restart, and quit) using the keys on the FPGA. Once the user selects a song, the FPGA retrieves the corresponding audio file, which contains the song’s instrumental track stored as a C array. The system then selects the corresponding lyrics file, which contains the expected vocal pitches, also stored as a C array. Once the song is complete, the system compares the user’s singing to the expected pitches and generates a score out of 100. Throughout the process, the song lyrics, and system status are displayed on the VGA monitor.


The pitch detection subsystem processes short windows of microphone data to estimate the fundamental frequency of the user’s singing voice. The design implements CMNDF (Cumulative Mean Normalized Difference Function) techniques. This approach improves pitch estimation accuracy while reducing octave detection errors commonly found in simpler autocorrelation-based methods.

Outcome

The project was successfully completed as part of a course at the University of Toronto. The project successfully demonstrates a fully functional FPGA-based karaoke system capable of performing pitch analysis, audio playback, lyric rendering, and scoring.
